A Muslim who prays the five daily prayers recites al-Fatihah at least seventeen times a day. Familiarity makes it easy to say the words without hearing them — and that is exactly what this reading is meant to undo.

## Why it is called the mother of the Book

The Prophet ﷺ called it *Umm al-Kitab*, the mother of the Book, because it contains in seven short verses what the rest of the Quran expands: praise of Allah, the relationship between servant and Lord, and the request for guidance that everything else answers.

## Verse by verse

The first three verses are pure praise. The fourth — *iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in* — is the hinge: worship and reliance handed over in one breath. The last three are a single request, repeated in a life that keeps needing it: show us the straight path.

Read it slowly in your next prayer. The words do not change; the posture does.
